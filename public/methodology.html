METHODOLOGY

What Hidden Conviction measures.

Hidden Conviction is a rules-based research signal built from public SEC filings. It looks for observable capital behavior and corporate events that may indicate unusually strong conviction.

Signal families

Insider Buying

Reported insider purchase activity, with context such as role, transaction size, and change in ownership.

Insider Clusters

Multiple insiders reporting purchase activity within the active scoring window.

Beneficial Ownership

Schedule 13D and 13G activity that reflects significant ownership or accumulation.

Institutional Confirmation

Quarterly 13F holdings can provide delayed confirmation of institutional accumulation. Because 13F data is reported after quarter end, it is treated as confirmation rather than a fresh standalone signal.

Corporate Events

Selected 8-K events are classified for their potential positive or negative contribution to conviction.

Signal Convergence

Independent signal families becoming active together can strengthen the overall conviction signal.

What is intentionally not disclosed

Hidden Conviction does not publish its proprietary component weights, thresholds, penalties, or scoring formula. The public product explains which signal families are active and links to the underlying SEC evidence so the source data can be independently reviewed.

Freshness policy

Public rankings only use a score materialized for the current UTC date. Positive scores are re-evaluated daily so rolling 30-day and 60-day evidence windows expire automatically. Company pages can show older history for research, but stale scores are explicitly labeled rather than treated as current.

Important limitations

SEC filings can be delayed, amended, incomplete, or difficult to compare across periods. Institutional holdings are especially stale by design. HCS is an informational research signal, not a prediction, valuation, recommendation, or personalized investment advice.
